# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Legal professionals, consultants, executives, and healthcare workers who handle sensitive conversations. They operate in environments where confidentiality is not a preference but a requirement — client meetings, board sessions, clinical consultations. When they reach for a meeting assistant, they need to know the tool takes discretion as seriously as they do. They are not early adopters; they are experts in their field who expect tools to stay out of the way.

## Product Purpose

Sonabrief is a privacy-first AI meeting assistant. Audio transcription runs entirely on-device via an in-browser Whisper model — audio never leaves the user's machine. Only the text transcript reaches the cloud for AI synthesis, and only when the user chooses cloud mode. The product's core promise is: the tool that handles your most sensitive conversations can be trusted to protect them. Success looks like professionals using Sonabrief without second-guessing whether the product is safe for their most important meetings.

## Brand Personality

Serious, calm, trustworthy. Precision without coldness. The product earns trust through competence, not reassurance. References: Linear (craft and clarity), ProtonMail (privacy commitment without paranoia). Voice is direct and expert — no filler, no enthusiasm, no exclamation marks.

## Anti-references

- **Notion / Coda**: casual, collaborative-wiki aesthetic — wrong register for confidential professional work
- **Generic SaaS cream**: soft gradients, rounded-everything, "friendly startup" warmth — communicates approachability, not trust
- **Otter.ai / Fireflies**: consumer meeting-recorder look — the exact visual territory Sonabrief must not occupy
- **Heavy enterprise UI**: Oracle/SAP density — intimidating, not calm

## Design Principles

1. **Privacy is structure, not decoration.** The UI should never look casual about security. Restraint and precision in layout choices communicate that this product handles serious data seriously.
2. **Earn trust through competence.** The interface signals expertise by being precise, not by being reassuring. No friendly copy, no tutorial hand-holding. The user is a professional.
3. **One surface, one job.** Each screen should have a single primary task. Navigation is infrastructure, not content.
4. **Visible only when needed.** UI elements earn their place by serving an active task. Decorative elements erode trust in a confidentiality product.
5. **Instrument-quality craft.** The product promises accurate transcription and exact meeting records. The design should match: intentional, measured, nothing arbitrary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Keyboard navigation and screen reader support expected for professional tool context. Reduced motion support required (many professionals use enterprise environments with OS-level accessibility settings).
